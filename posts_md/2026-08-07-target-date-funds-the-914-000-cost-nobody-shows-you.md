## Background

Meet Maya. She's 27, works in marketing in Austin, makes $62,000 a year. When she got hired, HR handed her a stack of forms, she checked the box for the 401(k), got her match, and felt like a responsible adult. Here's what she didn't do: pick an investment. So the plan did it for her — it dropped every dollar into a 'Target Retirement 2065 Fund.' And honestly? That's what happens to most of us. Fidelity and Vanguard data shows the majority of people under 35 hold basically one thing in their 401(k): the default target-date fund. Maya checked her balance last month — $31,000, growing every paycheck — and figured everything was fine. Then she actually looked at what the fund does. And here's the thing MarketWatch just called the biggest issue lurking inside these funds: it's not what the fund holds today. It's what the fund is programmed to do to her money over the next 38 years — without ever asking her.
