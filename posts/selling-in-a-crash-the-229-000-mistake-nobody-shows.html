The Hook

JPMorgan ran the numbers: $10,000 invested in the S&P 500 for 20 years grew to $64,844. But if you missed just the 10 best days — ten days out of twenty years — you ended with $29,708. Less than half. And here's the part nobody tells you: seven of those ten best days happened in the middle of crashes. The exact moment your gut screams 'sell' is the exact moment the market pays you the most for staying. Today I'm going to show you what panic selling actually costs — down to the dollar.

Background

Kiplinger just published a question that stopped me cold: what's your plan if the next pullback lasts years, not months? Most of us don't have one. So picture Maya. She's 28, makes $52,000 a year as a marketing coordinator, and she's done everything right — $15,000 sitting in a total market index fund inside her Roth IRA, plus $400 a month on auto-invest. Then the crash hits. Not a dip — a real one. Over eight brutal months, the market drops 40%. Her $15,000 becomes $9,000. Every headline says it's different this time. Her coworker sold everything last week and won't shut up about it. Her mom texts her an article titled 'Is This the End of the Stock Market As We Know It?' Maya opens her app at 11pm, thumb hovering over the sell button, thinking: 'I'll just get out now, wait until things feel safe again, and get back in.' That one decision — made in 30 seconds on a Tuesday night — is what we're pricing out today. Because 'wait until it feels safe' is the most expensive sentence in investing.
